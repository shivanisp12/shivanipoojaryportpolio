Finance training, applied to paperwork that decides outcomes




I completed an MBA in Finance and Analytics after a Bachelor of Commerce. My clearest hands-on experience is at an auto dealership, where I reviewed loan applications for income, repayment capacity and affordability, and compared terms across partner banks.


That work showed me where credit decisions slow down: missing documents, unclear income proof and teams that are not talking to each other. I enjoy fixing that kind of friction.




My projects cover the other side: pricing options with Black-Scholes across the top 20 NIFTY 50 stocks, and using regression in SPSS to find what drives satisfaction in a 182-response survey.


I am looking for roles in credit, risk, finance operations and analytics, where careful verification and clear numbers are the job. I have also led community and CSR work, which taught me to organise people and finish something concrete.

How I work




Start from the source
Check the document before trusting the summary. Loan files taught me that.


Keep assumptions visible
A number is only useful next to the inputs that produced it, which is why the lab shows every one.


Name what a model leaves out
Black-Scholes ignores dividends and early exercise. Saying so is part of the answer.
